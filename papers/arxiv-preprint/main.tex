\begin{abstract}
We introduce \hyphae, a cognitive substrate that composes responses
by verbatim quotation of retrieved memory fragments stitched
together with a curated connective lexicon, with no large language
model (LLM) in the cognition path. The architectural commitment
underlying the system is dual: every quoted body is preserved
byte-identically against a hash-chained substrate journal, making
each emitted response \emph{independently verifiable} against
named source material; and the realizer's bounded output runs in
microseconds on a single CPU core. We compare \hyphae{} against
18 LLM-based configurations (vanilla retrieval-augmented generation,
HyDE plus cross-encoder reranking, and oracle context) spanning
five generator models --- from Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepSeek, Meta,
and an in-house production router --- across two corpora: the
project's own 34-query composition corpus, and 150 random samples
from TriviaQA. We report two
complementary metrics: NLI-grounded
unsupported-claim rate (does the response stay inside the retrieved
context?) and gold-answer match (does the response contain the
benchmark's reference answer?). On TriviaQA, \hyphae{} reaches $1.000$ gold-answer match (by
construction of the corpus filter; see Section~\ref{sec:results-triviaqa});
in oracle mode, where the same gold-bearing seed body is supplied
to every system, frontier LLMs reach $0.840$--$0.960$ gold-answer.
The architectural finding is that verbatim quotation removes a
paraphrase-away failure mode that costs oracle-LLMs four to
sixteen percentage points of correctness. \hyphae{}'s NLI-grounded
unsupported-claim rate is a metric-degenerate $0.000$ on this
corpus; the twelve LLM-based configurations that reach gold-answer
$\geq 0.84$ score $0.400$--$0.737$ on the same metric. We trace
the gap mechanically to the metric's filter interacting with the
substrate's compositional template, and report both filtered and
unfiltered rates throughout. Where the architectural claim is
unambiguous is the cost dimension: on TriviaQA, \hyphae{}'s
realizer runs in $0.002$ ms mean per query, against $1{,}858$ ms
for a \emph{locally-hosted} Llama-8B-Instruct quantised baseline
(no network) and $2{,}424$ ms for GPT-4.1 with strong retrieval
via a hosted API --- a ratio of roughly $10^6$ in both cases,
confirming the network round-trip is not the dominant cost. On
the own corpus, whose multi-fragment queries make \hyphae{}'s
composition cost $12\times$ higher ($0.024$ ms mean), the ratio is
$\sim 10^5$. The advantage is therefore five-to-six orders of
magnitude depending on working-set size, and the \hyphae{} side
carries no model-serving infrastructure cost at all. We do not claim the substrate
universally replaces LLM-based generation. We claim that for tasks
in which the unit of grounding is a discrete retrieved fragment
quoted verbatim and the deployment requires audit-bound
verifiability, the architectural commitment is competitive with
frontier LLM correctness and dominates them on latency, memory
footprint, and per-query cost by several orders of magnitude. Code,
corpora, result envelopes, and hardware traces are available at
\url{https://github.com/terrizoaguimor/hyphae-v2}.
\end{abstract}
