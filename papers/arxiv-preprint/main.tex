\begin{abstract}
We introduce \hyphae, a cognitive substrate that composes responses
by verbatim quotation of retrieved memory fragments combined with a
curated connective lexicon, with no large language model (LLM) in
the cognition path. We compare \hyphae{} against 18 LLM-based
configurations (vanilla retrieval-augmented generation, HyDE plus
cross-encoder reranking, and oracle context) spanning five frontier
models from Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepSeek, and Meta. On a 150-query
TriviaQA standard-benchmark column, \hyphae{} achieves an
NLI-grounded unsupported-claim rate of $0.000$, while the closest
LLM-based system (DeepSeek-V4-Pro oracle) reaches $0.400$ --- a 40
percentage-point gap. \hyphae{}'s mean latency is $0.002$ ms versus
the LLM's $2{,}424$ ms, a $1.18\times10^6$ speedup. On the project's
own 34-query corpus the gap is narrower: GPT-4.1 with strong
retrieval ties \hyphae{} within bootstrap confidence intervals
($0.211$ vs $0.219$), but the latency advantage holds at
$\sim 5\times10^4$. We attribute this corpus-sensitivity to the
interaction between the metric's filter and \hyphae{}'s
compositional template. Ablations show that the lexicon-scale
component is the single observable driver of the metric deltas;
boundary smoothing, cascade-shape composition, and the ethics gate
each produce null deltas at this corpus size. We argue that the
production economics --- six orders of magnitude latency advantage
on commodity CPU --- generalise the architectural claim even where
NLI-quality measurements remain corpus-sensitive. Code, corpora,
result envelopes, and hardware traces are committed at
\url{https://github.com/terrizoaguimor/hyphae-v2}.
\end{abstract}
