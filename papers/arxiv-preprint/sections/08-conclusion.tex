\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented \hyphae{}, a cognitive substrate that
composes responses by verbatim quotation of retrieved memory
fragments stitched together with a curated connective lexicon
and produces an audit relation to a hash-chained substrate
journal. Every quoted body is byte-identical to a named source
fragment; the verification is mechanical and does not require
re-running the system.

On the TriviaQA-150 standard benchmark, \hyphae{} reaches a
$1.000$ gold-answer match rate by construction (the corpus
filter selects seed bodies that contain the answer; verbatim
quotation propagates the answer to the output). In oracle mode,
where the LLM baselines receive the same gold-bearing seed body,
they reach $0.840$--$0.960$ gold-answer match. The architectural
finding is therefore narrower than ``Hyphae is more correct'':
verbatim quotation removes a paraphrase-away failure mode that
costs oracle-LLMs four to sixteen percentage points of
correctness on this corpus. On the NLI-grounded unsupported-claim
metric, the headline $0.000$ vs $0.400$--$0.665$ delta
reflects partly a real architectural property and partly a
corpus-construction interaction with the chosen metric's
filter; we have made the mechanism precise and reported both
the filtered and unfiltered rates. The defensible quantitative
claim is therefore not that the architecture is dramatically
more truthful than frontier LLMs, but that it is
\emph{competitive on correctness while removing entire
infrastructure cost categories}.

The cost dimension is where the architectural commitment
matters unambiguously. \hyphae{}'s realizer runs in 2--20
microseconds on commodity CPU; the cheapest competitive
LLM-based configuration runs in approximately 1.7 seconds on
the same hardware with Metal acceleration. The ratio is
between four and six orders of magnitude depending on whether
the LLM is hosted locally or accessed via API, and it widens
by another factor of $\sim 7$ when GPU acceleration is
removed. Memory footprint differs by approximately five orders
of magnitude. For the subset of tasks in which the unit of
grounding is a discrete retrieved fragment quoted verbatim,
the trade-off is not between two systems of comparable cost;
it is between running on a single CPU core and provisioning
model-serving infrastructure.

The architectural claim is positional rather than universal.
\hyphae{}'s prose is template-rigid by design; the verbatim-
quotation commitment forbids paraphrastic composition.
Applications requiring fluent free-form generation need an
LLM. \hyphae{} is the right tool when the deployment values
verifiable grounding, sub-millisecond latency, or audit-bound
production economics --- and is willing to accept a
constrained prose register in exchange.

The full evaluation pipeline (\hyphae{} source, 19 comparator
configurations, two corpora, all result envelopes, hardware
traces, and this paper's source) is committed at
\url{https://github.com/terrizoaguimor/hyphae-v2}. Reproducing
the experimental matrix costs approximately USD 4 in inference
tokens at the LLM API layer; the \hyphae{} side has zero
cloud cost. The single largest open question this paper does
not address is reader preference: whether the template-rigid
prose is acceptable to end users on factual-retrieval queries
is a human-evaluation study, not a metric. We commit to that
followup as the next honest checkpoint.
